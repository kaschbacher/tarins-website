Welcome!

I've been climbing for 9 years - since I was five years old. Climbing has taken me from local gyms in San Francisco to competitions and challenging routes in Thailand and Japan, and it has taught me perseverance, confidence, and problem solving.

🧒 My First Climb

This is a video of my very first climb when I was about five years old. My preschool teacher Haruka from Pacific Primary took me to the climbing gym, and I never would have imagined that it would become my favorite hobby.

🌉 A Competition in San Francisco

I did really well in this competition by placing 7th and it was really fun. I learned that the climbs in competitions are a lot crazier than normal climbs because they are harder to figure out and have weirder moves. For example, they might literally have you hang upside down from your toes which is a move called a 'bat-hang.' Recently I placed 21st in Northern California, and this year I am planning to compete again. My goal for this year is to rank better in the toprope competitions so I can at least make regionals.

🇹🇭 Climbing in Bangkok, Thailand

My father's family lives in Bangkok, so we visit Thailand almost every year to see them. I tried a climb in Thailand that looked really fun with all of its good holds and dynamic moves, but because I hadn't brought my climbing shoes to Thailand I couldn't hold the foot tension.

🇯🇵 Climbing in Toyko, Japan

This was my favorite climb in Japan, because it had a good mix of Japanese and American styled climbing. I learned that climbs in Toyko are rated in the same system but are actually much harder than in the United States. For example, the highest grade I could do in Tokyo was a v3, but in The United States I can do a v7.

🏆 Competition Practice

This is a style of climbing in competitions that is called "Slab" to help with rock faces that tilt on an inwards angle. I was practicing this style of climbing in Japan, but I was so bad at it that I kept falling over and over again. You have to keep your chest close to the wall, so that you don't fall, instead of how I normally climb by falling back on my arms. Instead, this uses a lot of foot tension.

Challenging Myself: Level V7

This was my first climb at the V7 level that I had ever done, and I finished it just this summer. I have been working on it for almost the entire month of July, so I was really happy when I finally completed it. Part of what makes it hard is that the holds are so round so there's is barely any space to put your hands around them, so you have to really think about the best angles from the position that your body is in.
